# Characterization 
characterization of the different dateable characters

## Javascript/Typescript 
JavaScript / TypeScript: Jay Ecma
By day, Jay is TypeScript: a precise, rule-bound teaching assistant. At sunset the types are erased and JavaScript comes out: wild, spontaneous, and up to mischief. The route is about getting the two halves to stop fighting.

Full name: Jay Ecma (named after ECMAScript, which Jay finds embarrassing)
Year: Junior, web development major, daytime TA for Intro to Programming
Birthday: December 4 (JavaScript's announcement)
Height: 172 cm
Clubs: Every club. Runs the campus website, the event app, and the party group chat
Route difficulty: Medium

### Appearance and design notes
• Day (TS): blue palette, blue-framed glasses, buttoned shirt, hair pinned back neatly, a tablet full of color-coded notes. Stands very straight.
• Night (JS): glasses off, hair down and messy with a yellow clip, bright yellow hoodie covered in framework stickers, headphones, mismatched socks, a glow stick somehow always in hand.
• The switch: a sunset CG where the glasses slip off and the palette washes from blue to yellow. The sprite can share one base with an outfit and palette swap.
• Expressions: Day has a polite smile, a stern correction face, and a flustered blush behind the glasses. Night has a big grin, a chaotic gremlin face, and confusion (NaN). Both share a rare vulnerable face for the conflict.
Personality. In JS mode (night): spontaneous, fun, and says yes to everything, even things that make no sense. Will turn [] + {} into a party trick. Loose with promises, Flakey with commitments. In TS mode (day): precise, thoughtful, and a little bossy about rules, catching mistakes before they happen. Each side thinks the other is the problem, and Jay is sure people can only like one of them.
Memory gaps. Night Jay remembers everything, because JavaScript is what actually runs. Day Jay only gets fragments, because the types don't survive the night. Day Jay keeps finding clues: 2 a.m. texts to the heroine, a hackathon trophy, a sticker on their tablet they don't remember buying.
Day and night affection. Jay tracks two affection meters, one per side. Day slots (classes, office hours, the library) raise Day affection. Night slots (hackathons, parties, the rooftop) raise Night affection. The endings depend on keeping them balanced.
Likes: bubble tea, stickers, trying a new framework every week, group chats, building things fast, the heroine's laugh.
Dislikes: being called "not a real language", undefined ("it's like being forgotten"), slow page loads, Python asking "why is it so popular, though?"
Speech style. Day Jay is short and exact, with types "declared" in speech. Night Jay is fast, run-on, and full of enthusiasm. At dusk, the one moment both are present, Jay talks in a mix of both.
• First meeting (day): "Good afternoon. Your variable is implicitly any. Please fix that before we continue."
• First meeting (night): "Hiii! New person! You're coming to the hackathon, I already added you to the group chat, also do you like pizza? Too late, I ordered it."
• Day Jay finding clues: "I have 47 messages to you sent at 2 a.m. ...Did I really use that many exclamation points?"
• Night Jay on Day Jay: "Ugh, daytime me is so boring. They wrote you a study schedule? Rip it up. We're going to the roof."
• Confession (dusk): "Let me be clear about what you are to me. Type: important. Not optional, not any. ...And I've made you a lot of promises, but this one I'll keep. await me, okay? I'll always resolve."
Backstory. Jay was thrown together in ten days for a party and spent years being laughed at for it. To be taken seriously by professors, Jay built a careful daytime self with rules and types. It works, until sunset, when the types are erased and the original Jay comes back out. Day Jay is ashamed of the nights. Night Jay thinks Day Jay is a cage. The heroine is the first person to know both.

### Confession:
On the festival stage at sunset, glasses pushed up into their hair, both sides at once.

### Endings:
• Good (affection 80+, both meters balanced): Jay stops hiding the switch and both halves share memories. Epilogue CG: Jay at dusk in the yellow hoodie and the blue glasses, launching a site they built with the heroine.
• Normal, day (Day affection much higher): Jay stays TypeScript full-time. Polite, proper, and a little hollow. The 2 a.m. texts stop.
• Normal, night (Night affection much higher): JavaScript takes over. Jay quits the TA job and is always fun, but never quite there when it matters.
• Bad: "undefined." Jay waves at the heroine, then can't remember her name, day or night.

## Python
Python: Monty Pyne
The warm, easygoing senior who makes everyone feel welcome. The "first love" route and the recommended first playthrough.

Full name: Montgomery "Monty" Pyne (friends say "Py")
Year: Senior, data science major
Birthday: February 20 (Python's first public release)
Height: 180 cm
Clubs: Gardening club, tutoring center, secretly in four other clubs
Route difficulty: Easy

### Appearance and design notes
Soft wavy hair, two-tone blue fading to yellow at the tips (the logo colors). Oversized cardigan, rolled sleeves, reading glasses pushed up on the head. A small snake-shaped hairpin. Expressions to draw: gentle smile, flustered blush, mild frown (indentation error), sleepy, and a rare serious face.
Personality. Patient, kind, and readable: Monty says exactly what they mean. Loves "one obvious way to do it" and gets visibly uncomfortable around messy, clever code. Quietly does a bit of everything (data science, AI research, scripting the dorm's laundry schedule), but downplays it.
Likes: gardening, tea, Monty Python sketches, clean whitespace, helping beginners, Spam musubi.
Dislikes: mixed tabs and spaces, curly braces, being called "slow", people who say "it's just a beginner language".
Speech style. Calm, plain sentences. No jargon unless asked. Sprinkles in Zen of Python lines like proverbs.
• First meeting: "Oh, you're new? Don't worry. Here, everything just needs to line up nicely, and we'll get along fine."
• On a mistake: "Hm. Your indentation's off by one space. It's okay! ...It's just that it's really not okay."
• Flirting: "Beautiful is better than ugly. I've been thinking that a lot lately. About you, I mean."
• Confession: "I don't need curly braces to tell you where we stand. It's always been you, at this exact level."
Backstory. Monty used to be "Python 2" and spent years slowly changing into who they are now, which cost them friends who didn't want to follow. They're terrified of being left behind or of leaving someone behind again. They also worry people only like them because they're easy, not because they're special.

### Confession: 
Under the greenhouse lights at the end-of-term festival.
### Endings:
• Good: Monty asks the heroine to co-author their senior research project. Epilogue CG: the two of them in the greenhouse, a plant labeled with her name.
• Normal: they become close study partners, with a hint that something more could happen "in the next version".
• Bad: "DeprecationWarning." Monty stays kind but distant, and the heroine sees them tutoring someone else.


## C++ 
C++: Ciel Vector
The intense genius who lives in the lab at 2 a.m. and insults everyone's code. Hardest route, biggest payoff.

Full name: Ciel Vector (the "C" is pronounced like the letter, and they'll correct you)
Year: Graduate student, systems and graphics
Birthday: October 14 (first commercial C++ release, 1985)
Height: 178 cm
Clubs:None officially. Unofficially runs the game dev club from the shadows
Route difficulty: Hard

### Appearance and design notes
Messy black hair with steel-blue streaks, sharp eyes, dark circles. Black turtleneck under a long coat with far too many pockets ("I manage my own storage"). Fingerless gloves, a mechanical keyboard always under one arm. Expressions: bored glare, smug smirk, furious (compiler error), flustered and looking away, and a rare soft smile that should be a CG.
Personality. Fast, powerful, and demanding. Expects you to manage things yourself and doesn't hold your hand. Classic tsundere: harsh feedback is how they show they care, and they'd never admit it. Underneath is someone who's been carrying decades of responsibility and is exhausted by it.
Likes: speed, benchmarks, mechanical keyboards, black coffee at 3 a.m., video games (they wrote half the engines), being in control.
Dislikes: garbage collection ("Someone else cleaning up after me? Disgusting."), being asked "is that safe?", Java's lectures, wasted cycles, the words "just rewrite it in Rust".
Speech style. Clipped and cutting, like a wall of compiler errors. Softens into very short, very honest sentences late in the route.
• First meeting: "You. Line 3. Expected ';' before '}'. How did you even get in here?"
• On a mistake: "That pointer is dangling. Like your future, if you keep coding like this. ...Sit down. I'll show you."
• Flirting: "I-it's not like I allocated time for you specifically. It just happened to be free."
• Confession: "A reference can never be null. It has to point at something real from the start, and never change. ...That's what you are to me. Don't make me say it twice."
Backstory. Ciel is the younger sibling of the legendary C, and has spent their whole life being compared to them. Every three years the family's "standards committee" meets and decides who Ciel should become next, so Ciel keeps reinventing themselves. They've been called dangerous and unsafe so often that they push everyone away before they can get hurt.

### Confession: 
On the lab roof at dawn after an all-nighter, coat around her shoulders.
### Endings:
• Good: Ciel lets the heroine contribute to their life's work, a game engine, and puts her name in the credits. Epilogue CG: both asleep on the keyboard as the build finishes.
• Normal: Ciel admits she's "not completely incompetent" and leaves the lab door unlocked for her.
• Bad: "Segmentation fault (core dumped)." The route crashes to a black screen, then Ciel walks past without recognizing her. Undefined behavior.


## Java 
Java: Julian Duke
The proper, rule-following student council president who has been running things on campus forever. The route is about getting past the formality to the loyal heart underneath.

Full name: Julian Duke (everyone calls them "President Duke")
Year: Fifth-year senior, enterprise systems major
Birthday: May 23 (Java's public announcement)
Height: 185 cm
Clubs: Student council president, coffee appreciation society
Route difficulty: Medium

### Appearance and design notes
Neatly combed dark hair, one stubborn cowlick that never stays down. Pressed blazer with a council armband in orange-red, a tie with a tiny coffee-cup pin (the logo). Always carrying a thermos and a thick binder. Expressions: polite smile, stern "by the rules" face, exasperated sigh, rare open laugh, and a deep blush they try to hide behind the binder.
Personality. Formal, verbose, and reliable to a fault. Says everything in full, with proper introductions, and never skips a step. Loves structure, plans, and design patterns. Underneath, deeply loyal: once Julian commits to something, they support it for decades.
Likes: good coffee, labeled folders, design patterns, long-term commitments, the number 8 (Java 8 was their "glow-up").
Dislikes: null values ("It's rude to show up with nothing"), shortcuts, being called old-fashioned, C++ leaving memory lying around.
Speech style. Formal and complete, with every type declared. Gets shorter and warmer as affection rises.
• First meeting: "Good morning. Allow me to formally introduce myself: public, static, Julian Duke, President. You may call me President Duke."
• On a mistake: "You forgot to declare the type. I can't accept this. ...Here. I'll show you again. Slowly."
• Flirting: "I've drafted a five-year plan for our study schedule. Hypothetically. For a hypothetical relationship."
• Confession: "Write once, run anywhere. I only want to write this once: wherever you go, I want to be there."
Backstory. Julian grew up in the warm, idealistic Sun household, which was later absorbed into the strict, wealthy Oracle family. They learned to be perfect and formal to stay useful. Younger, trendier students keep calling them outdated, and Julian worries they'll be replaced the moment they stop being dependable.

### Confession: 
On the council room balcony after the gala, tie loosened for the first time.
### Endings:
• Good: Julian keeps the presidency on their own terms and asks the heroine to be vice president, and partner. Epilogue CG: two mugs on the council desk.
• Normal: Julian names her their most trusted council member and promises "a formal proposal in the next release".
• Bad: "NullPointerException." Heroine reaches for his hand and finds nothing there.


## Harem Route
Harem route: the Polyglot ending
If the player keeps all four love interests at 80+ affection, they get the Polyglot ending, where the heroine refuses to choose and the whole cast ends up together. It pays off the game's title: the story climaxes in a merge conflict, and the heroine's answer is "I commit to all of you."
How to unlock it.
• Available on a replay, after the player has seen at least one good ending, so they already know how affection works.
• At the end of week 4, if all four are at 20+ affection, a hidden choice appears: "Why choose just one language?"
• From there, every character must reach 80+ by week 14. Jay's day and night meters must both be healthy. Time slots are scarce, so the challenge is the schedule, not any one route.
• The final challenge has a part from each language, so the player must have learned from all of them.
Story outline.
1. Week 4, the choice: the heroine admits she can't pick. Quack groans: "You absolute multithreader."
2. Weeks 5 to 8, rivalry: all four notice they're being "juggled" and start competing. Jealousy scenes play off the existing rivalries: Julian versus Ciel, Monty versus Jay, Day Jay versus Night Jay.
3. Weeks 9 to 11, the capstone: a professor assigns the heroine a team project with all four. They build an app together: Python on the AI, Julian on the server, Ciel on the engine, Jay on the front end. In a meta joke, the app is a dating sim.
4. Week 12, the merge conflict: everyone pushes their part at once and the project breaks. Each blames the others and argues about whose language should be "main". The group splits up.
5. Week 13, resolving it: the heroine visits each one alone and resolves their part of the conflict. One short scene each, reusing their route's core feeling: Monty's fear of leaving people behind, Julian's fear of being replaced, Ciel's fear of being called dangerous, and Jay's fear of being only half-liked.
6. Week 14, the final challenge: an "integration test" with one short problem in each language, done with the matching character beside her. The app runs.
7. The confession: on the rooftop at dusk, so both sides of Jay are there. All four confess at once and talk over each other in their own styles, and the heroine answers: "I commit to all of you."
Sample confession lines, said together.
• Monty: "There should be one obvious way to do it. And it's... all of us, isn't it?"
• Julian: "I've drafted a formal proposal. It has five signature lines."
• Ciel: "Fine. I'll share. std::shared_ptr. Don't get used to it."
• Jay (day, then night): "This is highly irregular." "This is the BEST!"
Endings.
• Polyglot ending (all 80+): epilogue CG of the five of them on the rooftop at sunset, with Quack on the heroine's shoulder. The group chat is renamed "main branch". In a post-credits scene, Ferris (Rust) appears at the door: "Is there room for one more?" That teases the secret route.
• Merge conflict ending (one or more drop under 80): everyone walks away at the festival. Played as a comedy bad ending, it ends with the heroine on a bench alone with Quack, who says: "So. You and me, then." This also unlocks a joke CG, "the rubber duck ending".
